\begin{abstract}
Missing observations in spatial time series not only shorten temporal context but also make learned sensor relations unreliable. We propose MR-MSGF, a direct forecasting model that predicts from incomplete histories without a separately supervised imputation stage. MR-MSGF builds a window-specific relation graph whose learned similarities are regularized by pairwise co-observation, fuses it with physical topology, and coarsens the fused relation into physically connected regions. A lightweight reliability gate then controls when regional context should influence multi-scale prediction. On METR-LA, MR-MSGF yields consistent, paired-significant MAE reductions under contiguous and point-wise missingness. AQI36 native missingness and Block-T 70\% RMSE further characterize the mechanism as a missingness-aware bias rather than a cross-domain performance guarantee.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
Multivariate time series forecasting, missing observations, spatiotemporal graph learning, graph coarsening
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}

Traffic, environmental, and industrial forecasting systems depend on both temporal context and inter-sensor relations. Spatiotemporal graph models commonly use physical topology or learned adjacency to represent such relations \cite{dcrnn,mtgnn}, and hybrid designs show that physical and data-driven dependencies can be complementary \cite{stfgnn}. In real sensor networks, however, missing observations caused by faults or communication interruptions weaken two information sources at once: the observed temporal history of each sensor and the evidence used to infer pairwise relations.

A common response is to reconstruct missing history, using bidirectional recurrent imputation \cite{brits}, temporal attention for irregular samples \cite{mtan}, or graph-based imputation \cite{grin,spin}. Direct forecasting methods instead encode values and masks jointly \cite{crib,coifnet}. For graph forecasting under missingness, BiTGraph injects missing-pattern biases into temporal and graph propagation \cite{bitgraph}, GinAR combines predefined and adaptive relations when variable missingness damages dependency estimation \cite{ginar}, and GCN-M studies graph learning with incomplete traffic observations \cite{gcnm}. Hierarchical models aggregate information across spatial and temporal resolutions \cite{hdtts,pguts,higp}. These advances improve missing-aware representations, yet the above routes do not explicitly use window-level joint observation as edge evidence or condition regional context on node-specific reliability.

This paper studies relation reliability as a complementary issue to historical reconstruction. The key idea is simple: an edge inferred from two nodes that were rarely observed together should not be treated the same as an edge supported by dense co-observation. We therefore propose MR-MSGF, which directly forecasts from incomplete spatial history and regulates fine-to-coarse message passing by observation evidence. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We formulate \emph{relation reliability} as a window-level graph-learning condition: learned similarity is explicitly regularized by pairwise co-observation before sparse edge selection.
    \item We realize this condition through observation--physical relation fusion followed by physically connected regionalization, and use node-, edge-, and region-level evidence to control regional residual messages and multi-scale readout.
    \item We characterize both the support and boundary of the mechanism: paired METR-LA tests show consistent MAE reductions, whereas extreme contiguous missingness and AQI36 native missingness reveal that the bias is not uniformly beneficial.
\end{itemize}
MR-MSGF is complementary to imputation-first pipelines. Unlike adaptive graph methods that learn a relation from incomplete values alone, it exposes the amount of joint observation that supports each candidate edge. This distinction matters because the same learned similarity can have different reliability depending on how much of the two histories was actually observed.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Problem Setup and Backbone}

Let $X\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times N\times C}$ be a single history window from $N$ sensors and let $M\in\{0,1\}^{T\times N\times C}$ indicate observed entries. The model receives $X^{\obs}=M\odot X$, $M$, and a physical adjacency $A^{\phy}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}$, and predicts $Y\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times N\times C_y}$. In a mini-batch, relation graphs and reliability statistics are computed independently for each history window; we omit the batch index below. For graph construction, we use the channel-averaged mask $\bar M_{t,i}=C^{-1}\sum_cM_{t,i,c}$ and define
\begin{equation}
r_i=\frac{1}{T}\sum_t\bar M_{t,i},\qquad
q_{ij}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_t\bar M_{t,i}\bar M_{t,j}.
\end{equation}
Here $r_i$ is node availability and $q_{ij}$ is pairwise co-observation, and $\norm(A)_{ij}=A_{ij}/(\sum_{j'} A_{ij'}+\epsilon)$. To distinguish the proposed relation modeling from the existing temporal representation, a mask-aware encoder and a dilated recurrent hierarchy first produce $H_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}$, which is passed to the original spatial hierarchy to obtain multi-resolution states $B_{s,\ell}$. Here, $\ell$ indexes temporal levels, $s=0$ denotes the finest spatial level, and $s>0$ denotes the original coarser spatial levels. This hierarchy adopts established missingness-aware temporal modeling and multi-scale readout \cite{hdtts}; our relation branch modifies only $B_{0,\ell}$, while all $B_{s,\ell}$ for $s>0$ remain available to the readout.

Fig.~\ref{fig:mrmsgf-overview} outlines MR-MSGF: co-observation guides fine relations, whose regional context is reliability-gated for prediction.

\subsection{Observation-Supported Relation Fusion}

The encoder produces history features $E\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times N\times d}$. We obtain a window-specific node representation by masked pooling:
\begin{equation}
u_i=\frac{\sum_t\bar M_{t,i}E_{t,i}}{\sum_t\bar M_{t,i}+\epsilon}.
\end{equation}
The observation-supported score combines learned similarity and co-observation:
\begin{align}
s_{ij}&=\frac{(W_qu_i)^\top(W_ku_j)}{\sqrt d},\\
\tilde{s}_{ij}&=s_{ij}+\operatorname{softplus}(\beta)\log(q_{ij}+\epsilon).
\end{align}
Here $\beta$ is a learnable scalar. For each node, we retain the top-$k$ entries of $\tilde{s}_{ij}$, apply a row softmax, add self-loops, and row-normalize to obtain a window-specific directed sparse graph $A^{\obs}$; we set $k=8$ in all experiments. The logarithmic term is a soft evidence penalty: an edge with little joint observation is discouraged before sparsification, without imposing a hard correlation rule.

Let $\bar A^{\phy}$ denote the physical graph after adding self-loops and applying row normalization. A learned edge gate then forms the fine relation:
\begin{align}
g_{ij}&=\sigmoid\!\left(\psi_g([r_i,r_j,q_{ij},\bar A^{\phy}_{ij},A^{\obs}_{ij}])\right),\\
A^{\fine}&=\norm\!\left(g\odot\bar A^{\phy}+(1-g)\odot A^{\obs}\right).
\end{align}
Thus, both physical structure and observation-supported relations participate in end-to-end learned relation fusion. The gate is intentionally edge-specific rather than a global mixing coefficient, allowing physical and observation-supported evidence to receive different weights for different edges. In implementation, $\psi_g$ is a two-layer $5\!\rightarrow\!16\!\rightarrow\!1$ MLP with GELU activation.

\subsection{Reliability-Controlled Regional Context}

We construct a fixed hard assignment $S\in\{0,1\}^{N\times K}$ only from the physical graph, with each node assigned to exactly one region. We symmetrize the physical adjacency and remove self-loops, seed each region with the highest remaining physical-degree node, and greedily add the unassigned node with the largest positive affinity to any region member. A region stops growing if no such node exists or it reaches its maximum size $m$; we use $m=8$. This construction guarantees physical connectivity and does not group zero-affinity nodes merely to fill a region. With $n_k=\sum_iS_{ik}$, the fine state, regional state, and coarse relation are
\begin{align}
Z^{\fine}_{\ell}&=\operatorname{ELU}(A^{\fine}H_{\ell}W_f+H_{\ell}W_s),\\
H^{\reg}_{\ell,k}&=\frac{1}{n_k}\sum_iS_{ik}Z^{\fine}_{\ell,i},\\
A^{\coarse}&=\norm(S^\top A^{\fine}S).
\end{align}
Regional propagation and lifting are
\begin{align}
Z^{\reg}_{\ell}&=\operatorname{ELU}(A^{\coarse}H^{\reg}_{\ell}W_c+H^{\reg}_{\ell}W_r),\\
Z^{\coarse}_{\ell}&=SZ^{\reg}_{\ell}.
\end{align}
Coarsening after fine-scale fusion lets regional aggregation operate on a graph containing both physical structure and observation-supported relations; coarsening only the physical or observation graph cannot retain both sources of evidence.

For node $i$, let $\bar q_i=\sum_j\bar A^{\phy}_{ij}q_{ij}$ be physical-support-weighted co-observation, $\rho_i=\sum_kS_{ik}n_k^{-1}\sum_jS_{jk}r_j$ be regional coverage, and $b_i$ be the longest missing block in the current history window divided by $T$. Let $k(i)$ denote the region of node $i$, $c_{i,\ell}=H^{\reg}_{\ell,k(i)}$, and
\begin{align}
\zeta_{i,\ell}&=\sqrt{\max\!\left(d^{-1}\lVert Z^{\fine}_{\ell,i}-c_{i,\ell}\rVert_2^2,\epsilon\right)},\\
\delta_{i,\ell}&=\frac{\zeta_{i,\ell}}{N^{-1}\sum_j\zeta_{j,\ell}+\epsilon}.
\end{align}
Here $\delta_{i,\ell}$ is normalized representation dispersion from the regional center. The regional gate is
\begin{align}
\alpha_{i,\ell}=\sigmoid\big(&\psi_\alpha([r_i,\bar q_i,\rho_i,b_i,\delta_{i,\ell}])\\
&+\gamma^\top[1-r_i,1-\bar q_i,\rho_i-1,b_i,-\delta_{i,\ell}]\big).
\end{align}
Here $\psi_\alpha$ uses the same two-layer MLP structure as the edge gate, and $\gamma$ is a learnable scaling vector. The second term supplies missingness patterns and regional consistency as an additional residual signal. Since $\gamma$ is not sign-constrained, we do not interpret this term as a monotonic prior. Let $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\ell}=[\alpha_{1,\ell},\ldots,\alpha_{N,\ell}]^\top\in\mathbb{R}^N$, broadcast along the feature dimension. The finest representation becomes
\begin{equation}
\widetilde B_{0,\ell}=Z^{\fine}_{\ell}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\ell}\odot\big(Z^{\coarse}_{\ell}W_p\big),
\end{equation}
while $\widetilde B_{s,\ell}=B_{s,\ell}$ for $s>0$. We further bias multi-scale readout by reliability evidence. Let $R_i=[r_i,\bar q_i,\rho_i,b_i]^\top$. The score for prediction step $h$ is
\begin{equation}
e_{h,s,\ell,i}=e^{\mathrm{repr}}_{h,s,\ell,i}+w_{s,\ell}^\top R_i.
\end{equation}
For each node and prediction step, a softmax over $(s,\ell)$ yields $\pi_{h,s,\ell,i}$, and $F_{h,i}=\sum_{s,\ell}\pi_{h,s,\ell,i}\widetilde B_{s,\ell,i}$ is mapped to $\hat Y_{h,i}$ by the prediction head. Thus, reliability statistics bias scale selection rather than replacing representation content. Training minimizes masked forecasting MAE,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}}=\frac{\sum M^Y\odot|\hat Y-Y|}{\sum M^Y},
\label{eq:pred-loss}
\end{equation}
where $M^Y$ excludes unavailable future targets. We do not use reconstruction, graph, or clustering auxiliary losses.
The gate is not interpreted as calibrated uncertainty. It is a forecasting-oriented control variable that decides whether regional context should be added for a node at a temporal level. This design keeps the added branch lightweight while making regional messages conditional on missingness evidence rather than unconditional smoothing.

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Settings}

We evaluate on METR-LA traffic speeds and AQI36 air quality. All experiments forecast 12 steps from 24 historical steps. For METR-LA, Point and Block-T masks are applied only to the historical input; future targets are evaluated on valid, unmasked raw values. Point masks simulate independent random sensor-value loss, while Block-T masks simulate contiguous temporal outages. AQI36 retains its native validity mask and adds optional Point masks on top. Each condition uses five paired mask seeds, and compared models share the same input masks. The model is trained only with the masked forecasting loss in Eq.~\eqref{eq:pred-loss}, without imputation or graph auxiliary losses. We use $k=m=8$, initialize $\beta=1$ and $\gamma$ to ones, and obtain $K=31$ and $K=5$ physically connected regions for METR-LA and AQI36, respectively. HD-TTS-AMP is the main reference. METR-LA also includes BiTGraph connected to the common data, mask, and evaluation interface; because its original training schedule differs from HD-TTS, we interpret it only as a unified-protocol comparison.

\subsection{Main Results}

Table~\ref{tab:metr-main} reports METR-LA results. MR-MSGF lowers MAE over HD-TTS-AMP in all tested Block-T and Point settings. It also improves RMSE under Block-T 30\%/50\% and Point 30\%/50\%. At Block-T 70\%, MR-MSGF still improves MAE but has slightly worse RMSE, indicating that the benefit is not cross-metric consistent under extreme missingness. Across all five conditions, MR-MSGF wins all five paired MAE runs and two-sided paired $t$-tests give $p\leq0.0379$; we make no analogous across-condition significance claim for RMSE.

\subsection{Cross-Domain and Horizon Analysis}

AQI36 provides a cross-domain stress test with native missingness. Table~\ref{tab:aqi} shows that MR-MSGF is worse than HD-TTS-AMP under native missingness, improves both metrics with an additional 25\% Point mask, and lowers MAE but raises RMSE with an additional 50\% Point mask. The heterogeneous native missingness need not make the current reliability statistics align with transferable relations; this is a hypothesis suggested by the result, not a causal explanation. We therefore treat AQI36 as a boundary condition rather than claiming dataset-independent gains.

\section{Conclusion}

MR-MSGF forecasts incomplete spatial histories by making learned graph relations conditional on co-observation evidence, then fusing them with physical structure and gating regional context. The paired METR-LA results support relation reliability as a useful missingness-aware inductive bias. The mixed RMSE outcome at Block-T 70\% and AQI36 native-missingness result delimit this claim: the mechanism is not a cross-domain or cross-metric performance guarantee.
